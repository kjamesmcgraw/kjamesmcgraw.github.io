About

Research grounded in schools and communities

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Leadership and Policy Studies at Vanderbilt University’s Peabody College. I use quantitative, qualitative, and natural language processing methods to study school and district leaders, the policy and organizational contexts in which they work, and how policy contexts and policy implementation shape educational opportunity.

Before beginning doctoral study, I taught high school English in Jackson, Mississippi. I also served as an assistant principal with the Mississippi Teacher Corps, led professional learning for preservice and inservice teachers, and worked for the National Board for Professional Teaching Standards. My experience as a teacher and leader is at the core of the questions I ask.

As a teacher, I experienced how policies intended to support students, educators, and educational opportunity can produce a range of outcomes once they encounter organizational routines and unequal distributions of power and resources. I saw how leaders’ decisions when navigating organizational processes and policy systems expanded or constrained students' educational opportunities and sense of belonging in schools, as well as the working conditions and career trajectories of educators. My classroom experience impressed upon me the historical, social, and political importance of centering the American South and the experiences of Black students and educators in racially and socioeconomically segregated systems.
