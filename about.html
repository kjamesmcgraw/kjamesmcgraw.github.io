About

Research grounded in schools and communities

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Leadership and Policy Studies at Vanderbilt University’s Peabody College. I use quantitative, qualitative, and natural language processing methods to study school and district leaders, the policy and organizational contexts in which they work, and the equity implications of their decisions.

Before beginning doctoral study, I taught high school English in Jackson, Mississippi. I also served as an assistant principal with the Mississippi Teacher Corps, led professional learning for preservice and inservice teachers, and worked for the National Board for Professional Teaching Standards. My experience as a teacher and leader is at the core of the questions I ask.

As a teacher, I experienced how policies intended to support students, educators, and educational opportunity can produce a range of outcomes once they encounter organizational routines and unequal distributions of power and resources. I saw the power of leaders’ decisions in navigating organizational processes and policy constraints, working for principals who denied students opportunities and autonomy in an attempt to comply with state policy—and principals who subverted formal policies to better affirm and empower their staff and students. My classroom experience also left me convicted about the historical, social, and political importance of focusing on the American South and the necessity of centering the experiences of Black students and educators in racially and socioeconomically segregated systems.
